# Periodic-Table

## I. Fundamental Atomic Structure and Shell Organisation
### A. Basic Atomic Components and Nucleus
#### The Nucleus and its Composition
##### a. The nucleus is located at the spherical centre of the atom
##### b. It contains protons and neutrons; the atomic number ($Z$) represents the number of protons
##### c. In a neutral atom, the number of protons equals the number of electrons
#### Electronic Motion
##### a. Electrons rotate in designated shells outside the nucleus
##### b. The primary designation for these shells is the Principal Quantum Number ($n$)
### B. Multi-level Shell Classification
#### The Ultimate Shell
##### a. Represented by the $n^{th}$ shell
##### b. Also referred to as the "outermost shell"
##### c. Conceptually represents the "ultimate goal," similar to an IIT selection
#### The Penultimate Shell
##### a. Represented as the $(n-1)^{th}$ shell
##### b. It is the shell immediately inside the outermost layer
#### The Anti-penultimate Shell
##### a. Represented as the $(n-2)^{th}$ shell
##### b. It is the shell located two layers inside the ultimate shell
### C. Subshells and Orbital Capacity
#### Subshell Types and Orbital Counts
##### a. The $s$ subshell contains 1 orbital; $p$ contains 3; $d$ contains 5; $f$ contains 7
##### b. Each orbital can accommodate a maximum of two electrons based on the Pauli Exclusion Principle
#### Maximum Electron Capacities
##### a. $s$ = 2 electrons; $p$ = 6 electrons
##### b. $d$ = 10 electrons; $f$ = 14 electrons
## II. The Aufbau Principle and Electronic Configuration
### A. The "Building Up" Energy Sequence
#### Mnemonic for Energy Ordering
##### a. The sequence is: $s, sp, sp, sdp, sdp, sfdp, sfdp$
##### b. This represents the order in which electrons fill various subshells
#### Subshell-Principal Quantum Number Relationship
##### a. When filling $d$ subshells, the principal quantum number is $n-1$ relative to $s$ and $p$
##### b. When filling $f$ subshells, the principal quantum number is $n-2$ relative to $s$
### B. Mathematical Derivation of Periodic Structure
#### Magic Numbers and Element Distribution
##### a. The number of elements per period follows the sequence: 2, 8, 8, 18, 18, 32, 32
##### b. These numbers are derived from the maximum electron capacity of the subshell sets in the energy sequence
#### Determining Period Numbers
##### a. The period number of an element corresponds to the maximum Principal Quantum Number ($n$) in its configuration
### C. Modern Periodic Table Block Division
  - $s$-Block and $p$-Block
##### a. $s$-block consists of groups 1 and 2; $p$-block consists of groups 13 to 18
##### b. Elements are placed in these blocks based on where their last electron enters
  - $d$-Block and $f$-Block
##### a. $d$-block spans groups 3 to 12; $f$-block elements (Lanthanides and Actinides) are technically part of Group 3
##### b. The $f$-block is separated at the bottom to maintain the table's symmetry
## III. Classification and Characterisation of Elements
### A. Metallic and Non-Metallic Divisions
#### Metals
##### a. Entire $s$, $d$, and $f$ blocks are metallic, along with some $p$-block elements
##### b. Metals comprise approximately 72% of the periodic table
##### c. Characterised by the tendency to donate electrons and form cations
#### Non-Metals and Metalloids
##### a. Non-metals, such as Hydrogen, Carbon, and Oxygen, tend to receive electrons
##### b. Metalloids (e.g., $As, Sb, Te, Ge, Si$) possess both metallic and non-metallic traits
##### c. A mnemonic for metalloids is "Aaj Sab Teji"
### B. Specific Elemental Families
#### Main Group and Representative Elements
##### a. Main Group: $s$ and $p$ block elements
##### b. Representative: $s$ and $p$ block excluding Group 18
#### Named Groups
##### a. Group 1: Alkali Metals; Group 2: Alkaline Earth Metals
##### b. Group 15: Pnictogens; Group 16: Chalcogens; Group 17: Halogens
### C. Transition and Inner Transition Elements
#### Transition Elements ($d$-block)
##### a. Defined by partially filled $d$-orbitals in their atomic or common ionic states
##### b. Group 12 ($Zn, Cd, Hg$) are $d$-block elements but not considered transition metals as they have full $d^{10}$ configurations
#### Inner Transition Elements ($f$-block)
##### a. Comprised of the $4f$ series (Lanthanides, $Z=58-71$) and $5f$ series (Actinides, $Z=90-103$)
## IV. IUPAC Nomenclature for Elements with $Z > 100$
### A. Systematic Naming Rules
#### Numerical Roots
##### a. 0=nil, 1=un, 2=bi, 3=tri, 4=quad, 5=pent, 6=hex, 7=sept, 8=oct, 9=enn
#### Suffix and Symbol Formation
##### a. The suffix "-ium" is added to the end of the combined roots
##### b. The symbol is formed using the first letter of each root (e.g., $Z=101$ is Unnilunium, symbol $Unu$)
### B. Historical Naming Mnemonic (101-118)
#### Mnemonic Phrase
##### a. "Mendeleev Ko Mila Nobel In Lawrence; Rutherford Doobe Sagar Mein, Bohr Has Mat; Darr Se Roye Copper; Niho Fly To Moscow To Live In Ten-Again"
#### Correlation to Atomic Numbers
##### a. $101$: Mendelevium; $102$: Nobelium; $103$: Lawrencium
##### b. $104$: Rutherfordium; $112$: Copernicium; $118$: Oganesson (symbol $Og$)
## V. Periodic Trend: Effective Nuclear Charge and Screening
### B. Effective Nuclear Charge ($Z_{eff}$)
#### The $Z_{eff}$ Formula
##### a. $Z_{eff} = Z - \sigma$, where $Z$ is nuclear charge and $\sigma$ is the shielding constant
##### b. Represents the actual force of attraction felt by an outer electron
#### The Role of the Screening/Shielding Constant ($\sigma$)
##### a. Innershell electrons act as a "shield" or "screen" between the nucleus and outer electrons
##### b. The value of $\sigma$ depends on the number of electrons in the inner shells
### B. Penetration and Shielding Power
#### Penetration Effect
##### a. Refers to how close a subshell can get to the nucleus
##### b. Order of closeness: $s > p > d > f$
#### Shielding/Screening Efficiency
##### a. $s$-orbitals are the best shields because they are spherical and closest to the nucleus
##### b. $d$ and $f$ orbitals provide "poor shielding" due to their diffused shapes
## VI. Periodic Trend: Atomic and Ionic Radii
### A. Types of Atomic Radii
#### Covalent and Metallic Radii
##### a. Covalent: Measured in non-metals through shared electron pairs
##### b. Metallic: Measured between touching metal atoms in a lattice
#### Van der Waals Radius
##### a. Measured between non-bonded atoms or molecules
##### b. Always the largest of the three types due to lack of bonding overlap
### B. General Trends and Anomalies
#### Group and Period Variations
##### a. Left to Right (Period): Atomic size decreases as $Z_{eff}$ increases
##### b. Top to Bottom (Group): Atomic size increases due to the addition of new shells
#### Specific Anomalies
##### a. Gallium ($Ga$) has a smaller atomic radius than Aluminium ($Al$) due to poor shielding by $3d$ electrons
##### b. Noble gases (Group 18) appear exceptionally large because their van der Waals radii are measured instead of covalent radii
### C. Lanthanide and Actinide Contractions
#### Lanthanide Contraction
##### a. A significant decrease in size across the $4f$ series due to very poor shielding of $4f$ electrons
##### b. Causes the sizes of $4d$ and $5d$ series elements (like $Zr$ and $Hf$) to be nearly identical
#### Actinide Contraction
##### a. A similar size decrease across the $5f$ series due to poor shielding of $5f$ electrons
### D. Ionic Radius Rules
#### Cations vs. Anions
##### a. Cations are always smaller than their parent atoms because $Z_{eff}$ increases
##### b. Anions are always larger than their parent atoms due to increased electron-electron repulsion
#### Isoelectronic Species
##### a. Species with the same number of electrons (e.g., $N^{3-}, O^{2-}, F^-, Na^+, Mg^{2+}$)
##### b. For these, size decreases as the atomic number (number of protons) increases
## VII. Periodic Trend: Ionisation Energy (IE)
### A. Definition and Successive Values
#### The Basic Process
##### a. The minimum energy required to remove an electron from an isolated gaseous atom
##### b. It is an endothermic process (energy must be supplied)
#### Successive Ionisation Energies
##### a. $IE_1 < IE_2 < IE_3$ always
##### b. Removing a second electron is harder because it must be taken from a positively charged cation with a higher $Z_{eff}$
### B. Primary Influencing Factors
#### Atomic Size and $Z_{eff}$
##### a. IE is inversely proportional to atomic size; larger atoms have lower IE
##### b. IE is directly proportional to $Z_{eff}$
#### Subshell Stability
##### a. Half-filled ($p^3, d^5, f^7$) and fully-filled ($s^2, p^6, d^{10}, f^{14}$) configurations are extra stable
##### b. Nitrogen has a higher $IE_1$ than Oxygen because Nitrogen has a stable half-filled $2p^3$ configuration
### C. Important Anomalies and Trends
#### Penetration Anomalies
##### a. Beryllium ($2s^2$) has a higher IE than Boron ($2p^1$) because $s$-electrons are more penetrated (closer to the nucleus) than $p$-electrons
#### Boron Family Trends
##### a. Follows a "W-shaped" trend: $B > Tl > Ga > Al > In$
##### b. Thallium has a high IE due to the lanthanide contraction and poor $4f$ shielding
## VIII. Periodic Trend: Electron Gain Enthalpy and Affinity
### A. Concepts of Energy Change
#### Exothermic vs. Endothermic
##### a. For most atoms, the first electron gain is exothermic (energy is released)
##### b. For stable atoms (Noble gases, $Be, Mg, N$), it is endothermic (energy must be absorbed)
#### Successive Electron Gain
##### a. The second electron gain enthalpy ($\Delta_{eg}H_2$) is always positive
##### b. Adding an electron to a negative ion requires energy to overcome electrostatic repulsion
### B. Key Trends and Group 17 Dominance
#### Group 17 (Halogens)
##### a. Have the most negative electron gain enthalpies
##### b. Chlorine has a more negative value than Fluorine because Fluorine’s small size leads to high inter-electronic repulsion in its $2p$ subshell
#### Group 16 (Chalcogens)
##### a. Sulphur has the most negative electron gain enthalpy in the group
##### b. Oxygen has the least negative value in its group due to its extremely small size
## IX. Periodic Trend: Electronegativity and Chemical Properties
### A. Nature of Electronegativity
#### Definition and Measurement
##### a. The qualitative ability of an atom in a molecule to attract shared electrons toward itself
##### b. Measured on the Pauling scale, where Fluorine is the highest (4.0)
#### Trends in the Table
##### a. Increases from Left to Right across a period
##### b. Decreases from Top to Bottom down a group
### B. Diagonal Relationship
#### The Concept
##### a. Similarity in properties between certain elements in the 2nd and 3rd periods that are diagonally related
##### b. Examples include $Li-Mg$, $Be-Al$, and $B-Si$
#### Underlying Cause
##### a. These pairs have similar ionic sizes and similar charge/radius ratios
## X. Nature of Oxides and Moseley’s Law
### A. Classification of Oxides
#### Basic and Acidic Oxides
##### a. Metallic oxides (low oxidation state) are generally basic
##### b. Non-metallic oxides (and metals in high oxidation states like $+6, +7$) are acidic
#### Amphoteric and Neutral Oxides
##### a. Amphoteric (react with both acids and bases): Mnemonic "Aaj Sab CarVento 13; Jaan Suno Be Alia Pub Gayi"
##### b. Neutral (react with neither): $CO, NO, N_2O$
### B. Moseley’s Equation
#### The Relationship
##### a. $\sqrt{\nu} = a(Z - b)$, where $\nu$ is frequency, $Z$ is atomic number, and $a, b$ are constants
#### Significance
##### a. Proved that the atomic number, not atomic mass, is the fundamental property of elements
