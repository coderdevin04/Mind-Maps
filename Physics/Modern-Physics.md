# Modern-Physics

## I. DUAL NATURE OF RADIATION AND MATTER
### A. The Photoelectric Effect
#### 1.  Fundamental Definitions
##### a.  Phenomenon: The emission of electrons from a metal surface when light of a certain frequency falls on it
##### b.  Threshold Frequency ($\nu_0$): The minimum frequency required to initiate the experiment; below this, no emission occurs regardless of intensity
##### c.  Work Function ($\Phi_0$): The minimum energy required to remove an electron from the metal surface ($\Phi_0 = h\nu_0$); this value is metal-dependent
##### d.  Cut-off Wavelength ($\lambda_0$): The maximum wavelength up to which the photoelectric effect will occur ($\Phi_0 = hc/\lambda_0$)
#### 2.  Experimental Observations
##### a.  Instantaneous Process: Emission occurs within $10^{-9}$ seconds, contradicting wave theory which predicted an energy accumulation time lag
##### b.  Intensity Dependence:
  - i.   Intensity is defined as energy per unit area per unit time (related to the number of photons)
  - ii.  Increasing intensity increases the number of emitted photoelectrons (Photo Current), provided frequency is above threshold
  - iii. Intensity does not affect the kinetic energy or stopping potential
##### c.  Frequency Dependence:
  - i.   Frequency determines the kinetic energy of the emitted electrons
  - ii.  Increasing frequency increases the Stopping Potential required to halt electrons
#### 3.  Graphical Analysis
##### a.  Photo Current vs. Intensity: Linear relationship (straight line through origin)
##### b.  Photo Current vs. Potential (Voltage):
  - i.   Saturation Current: Maximum constant current reached at positive collector voltage
  - ii.  Stopping Potential ($V_s$): Minimum negative voltage to stop the most energetic electrons (current becomes zero)
  - iii. Variation: Different intensities have different saturation currents but same $V_s$ (if frequency constant); different frequencies have same saturation current but different $V_s$ (if intensity constant)
#### 4.  Einstein’s Particle Theory (Photon Theory)
##### a.  Quantization: Light consists of discrete energy packets called photons ($E = h\nu$)
##### b.  Interaction: One photon interacts with one electron in an instantaneous process
##### c.  Photoelectric Equation:
  - i.   Energy Balance: Incident Energy = Work Function + Max Kinetic Energy ($h\nu = \Phi_0 + K_{max}$)
  - ii.  Alternate Forms:*   $hc/\lambda = hc/\lambda_0 + \frac{1}{2}mv^2_{max}$ 18.*   $h\nu = \Phi_0 + e V_s$ (using Stopping Potential)
##### d.  Efficiency: The number of emitted electrons is usually less than the number of incident photons (Efficiency < 100%) due to internal collisions
### B. Matter Waves (De Broglie Hypothesis)
#### 1.  Core Concept
##### a.  Dual Nature: Just as light has particle nature, matter particles (mass $m$) moving with velocity $v$ have an associated wave nature
##### b.  Wavelength Formula: $\lambda = h/p = h/mv$
#### 2.  Formulas for Specific Cases
##### a.  Kinetic Energy (K): $\lambda = h / \sqrt{2mK}$
##### b.  Charged Particles (Accelerated by $V$): $\lambda = h / \sqrt{2mqV}$
  - i.   Electron: $\lambda = 12.27 / \sqrt{V}$ $\mathring{A}$
  - ii.  Proton: $\lambda = 0.286 / \sqrt{V}$ $\mathring{A}$
  - iii. Alpha Particle: $\lambda = 0.101 / \sqrt{V}$ $\mathring{A}$
##### c.  Thermal Neutrons/Gas: $\lambda = h / \sqrt{fmkT}$ (where $f$ is degrees of freedom; e.g., $3mkT$ for monoatomic)
#### 3.  Advanced Applications
##### a.  In Electric Fields: Velocity changes ($v = u + at$), causing $\lambda$ to vary with time
##### b.  In Magnetic Fields: Particle moves in a circle ($r = mv/qB$); wavelength is $\lambda = h/qBr$
##### c.  Collisions/Explosions: Conservation of linear momentum is used to determine the wavelength of resulting fragments
### C. Radiation Pressure and Force
#### 1.  Mechanism: Light carries momentum ($p = E/c$); reflection or absorption causes momentum change, exerting force
#### 2.  Force Formulas (Normal Incidence)
##### a.  Perfectly Absorbing: $F = IA/c$ (Momentum change $p$) 33.b.  Perfectly Reflecting: $F = 2IA/c$ (Momentum change $2p$)
#### 3.  Oblique Incidence (Angle $\theta$)
##### a.  Reflecting: Force acts perpendicular to the surface; $F = (2IA \cos^2\theta) / c$
##### b.  Absorbing: Force has components; magnitude $IA/c$
#### 4.  Sphere: For a sphere, the force is $F = IA/c$ (using projected area $\pi R^2$) regardless of reflection or absorption
## II. ATOMS
### A. Rutherford’s Model
#### 1.  Alpha Scattering Experiment
##### a.  Observations: Most alpha particles passed undeflected (empty space); very few deflected >90° (heavy, dense nucleus)
##### b.  Inference: Positive charge and mass are concentrated in a tiny central nucleus
#### 2.  Key Parameters
##### a.  Distance of Closest Approach ($r_0$): Point where Kinetic Energy converts entirely to Electrostatic Potential Energy
##### b.  Impact Parameter ($b$): Perpendicular distance of velocity vector from nucleus center; smaller $b$ implies larger scattering angle
#### 3.  Drawbacks: Could not explain stability (accelerating electron should radiate) or discrete spectral lines
### B. Bohr’s Model (Hydrogen-like species)
#### 1.  Postulates
##### a.  Stationary Orbits: Electrons revolve in stable orbits without radiating energy
##### b.  Force Balance: Electrostatic attraction provides centripetal force ($kZe^2/r^2 = mv^2/r$)
##### c.  Quantization: Angular momentum is quantized ($mvr = nh/2\pi$)
##### d.  Transitions: Radiation is emitted/absorbed only during transitions between orbits ($E_2 - E_1 = h\nu$)
#### 2.  Derived Properties (Proportionalities)
##### a.  Radius ($r_n$): $\propto n^2/Z$ ($r_n = 0.529 n^2/Z$ $\mathring{A}$)
##### b.  Velocity ($v_n$): $\propto Z/n$ ($v_n = 2.18 \times 10^6 Z/n$ m/s)
##### c.  Energy ($E_n$): $\propto Z^2/n^2$ ($E_n = -13.6 Z^2/n^2$ eV)
##### d.  Time Period ($T$): $\propto n^3/Z^2$
##### e.  Magnetic Field ($B$): $\propto Z^3/n^5$
#### 3.  Spectral Lines
##### a.  Emission: Electron moves High $\rightarrow$ Low energy (bright lines); Absorption: Low $\rightarrow$ High (dark lines)
##### b.  Rydberg Formula: $1/\lambda = R Z^2 (1/n_1^2 - 1/n_2^2)$
##### c.  Series: Lyman ($n_1=1$, UV), Balmer ($n_1=2$, Visible), Paschen ($n_1=3$, IR) 53.d.  Extremes:
  - i.   $\lambda_{max}$ (Min Energy): Transition $n_1+1 \rightarrow n_1$
  - ii.  $\lambda_{min}$ (Series Limit): Transition $\infty \rightarrow n_1$
#### 4.  Recoil: Atom recoils when emitting a photon to conserve momentum
## III. NUCLEI
### A. Structure
#### 1.  Composition: Nucleus contains Protons ($Z$) and Neutrons ($N$); Mass Number $A = Z+N$
#### 2.  Size: Radius $R = R_0 A^{1/3}$ ($R_0 \approx 1.2$ fm); Volume $\propto A$
#### 3.  Density: Constant ($\approx 10^{17}$ kg/m$^3$) for all nuclei, independent of mass number $A$
### B. Forces and Stability
#### 1.  Nuclear Force
##### a.  Properties: Short-range, strongest force, charge-independent, spin-dependent, non-central
##### b.  Saturation: Interacts only with nearest neighbors
#### 2.  Binding Energy (BE)
##### a.  Mass Defect ($\Delta m$): Difference between sum of nucleon masses and actual nuclear mass
##### b.  Energy Relation: $BE = \Delta m \cdot c^2$ (Conversion: 1 amu $\approx$ 931.5 MeV)
##### c.  Stability Curve ($BE/A$):
  - i.   Higher BE per nucleon ($BE/A$) implies greater stability
  - ii.  Iron (Fe) is most stable; light nuclei fuse (Fusion) and heavy nuclei split (Fission) to increase $BE/A$
##### d.  Q-Value: Energy released in reaction = $(Mass_{reactants} - Mass_{products})c^2$
## IV. RADIOACTIVITY
### A. Basic Principles
#### 1.  Definition: Spontaneous disintegration of unstable nuclei to attain stability
#### 2.  Law of Decay:
##### a.  Rate of decay is proportional to number of nuclei ($-dN/dt = \lambda N$)
##### b.  Equation: $N = N_0 e^{-\lambda t}$
##### c.  Activity ($A$): $A = \lambda N$; decays exponentially ($A = A_0 e^{-\lambda t}$)
#### 3.  Life Parameters:
##### a.  Half-Life ($T_{1/2}$): Time for $N$ to become $N_0/2$; $T_{1/2} = \ln(2)/\lambda$
##### b.  Mean Life ($\tau$): Average life of nuclei; $\tau = 1/\lambda$
### B. Types of Decay
#### 1.  Alpha Decay ($\alpha$):
##### a.  Emission of Helium nucleus (${2}He^4$) 75.b.  Kinetic Energy of $\alpha$ particle: $K\alpha = \frac{A-4}{A} Q$
#### 2.  Beta Decay ($\beta$):
##### a.  $\beta^-$ Decay: Neutron converts to Proton + Electron + Antineutrino
##### b.  $\beta^+$ Decay: Proton converts to Neutron + Positron + Neutrino
#### 3.  Gamma Decay ($\gamma$): Emission of EM radiation from excited nucleus; no change in $Z$ or $A$
