# Electric-Charge

## I. Electric Charges
### A. Fundamental Definition and Nature
#### 1. Definition
##### a. A property of matter that allows it to exert electrical or Columbian force on other bodies
##### b. It is always associated with mass; there is no charge without mass
#### 2. Types of Charges
##### a. Positive charges (e.g., protons)
##### b. Negative charges (e.g., electrons)
#### 3. Neutrality
##### a. A body is neutral when it has equal amounts of positive and negative charge
##### b. Charging is achieved by disturbing this neutrality (adding or removing electrons)
### B. Fundamental Properties
#### 1. Scalar Quantity
##### a. Charge has magnitude but no direction; it adds algebraically (e.g., $q_1 + q_2$)
#### 2. Quantization of Charge
##### a. Charge exists in discrete packets, not continuous values
##### b. Formula: $Q = \pm ne$, where $n$ is an integer and $e$ is the charge of an electron ($1.6 \times 10^{-19}$ C)
#### 3. Conservation of Charge
##### a. Charge can neither be created nor destroyed, only transferred
#### 4. Invariance
##### a. Charge does not change with speed, unlike mass, length, or time
##### b. The value of charge remains constant regardless of the frame of reference
## II. Methods of Charging
### A. Friction (Rubbing)
#### 1. Mechanism
##### a. Transfer of valence electrons from one body to another due to rubbing
##### b. The body gaining electrons becomes negative (mass increases); the body losing electrons becomes positive (mass decreases)
#### 2. Applicability
##### a. Works for both insulators and conductors
##### b. Example: Combing hair or rubbing a plastic scale
### B. Electrostatic Induction
#### 1. Mechanism
##### a. Charging without physical contact by bringing a charged body near a neutral one
##### b. Causes polarization (redistribution of charges) within the neutral body
#### 2. Role of Earthing
##### a. Connecting a conductor to the earth sets its potential to zero
##### b. Electrons flow from earth to body (if body is positive) or body to earth (if body is negative) to neutralize the potential
#### 3. Applicability
##### a. Only possible for conductors where electrons are free to move
### C. Conduction (Contact)
#### 1. Mechanism
##### a. Direct physical contact between a charged conductor and an uncharged conductor
##### b. Charge distributes until potential is the same across both bodies
#### 2. Charge Sharing Formula
##### a. For identical spheres, total charge divides equally: $Q_{final} = (q_1 + q_2) / 2$
##### b. Charge distribution is directly proportional to radius
## III. Coulomb’s Law and Electric Force
### A. The Law
#### 1. Formula and Dependence
##### a. Force is directly proportional to the product of charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance: $F = k \frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2}$
##### b. Depends on the medium between charges
#### 2. Conditions
##### a. Strictly applicable only for point charges
##### b. Bodies can be treated as point charges if their dimensions are negligible compared to the distance between them
### B. Medium Dependence (Dielectric Constant)
#### 1. Permittivity
##### a. $\epsilon_0$ (vacuum) $\approx 8.85 \times 10^{-12}$ units; Dimension: $M^{-1}L^{-3}T^4A^2$
##### b. Force in a medium is reduced by a factor of $K$ (Dielectric constant): $F_{medium} = F_{vacuum} / K$
#### 2. Fractional Change
##### a. Fractional change in force when moved to a medium: $(K-1)/K$
### C. Properties of Electric Force
#### 1. Nature of Force
##### a. Central force: Acts along the line joining the centers of charges
##### b. Conservative: Work done in a closed path is zero
##### c. Action-Reaction Pair: Forces are equal and opposite (Newton’s 3rd Law)
#### 2. Superposition Principle
##### a. Net force on a charge is the vector sum of individual forces from all other charges
##### b. Individual forces are unaffected by the presence of other charges
### D. Equilibrium and Null Points
#### 1. Like Charges (e.g., $+q, +nq$)
##### a. Null point (Force = 0) lies between the charges
##### b. Location: $x = \frac{d}{\sqrt{n} + 1}$ from the smaller charge
#### 2. Unlike Charges (e.g., $+q, -nq$)
##### a. Null point lies outside the line joining charges, towards the smaller charge
##### b. Location: $x = \frac{d}{\sqrt{n} - 1}$ from the smaller charge
#### 3. Time Period of Oscillation (SHM)
##### a. For a charge slightly displaced along the axis of a charged ring or between fixed charges
##### b. Formula: $T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{m r^3}{k q q_0}}$ (assuming small displacement $x \ll r$)
## IV. Electric Field (Intensity)
### A. Definition and Concept
#### 1. Concept
##### a. The 3D space around a charge where it exerts force on other charges
#### 2. Intensity Formula
##### a. Force experienced per unit positive test charge: $\vec{E} = \vec{F} / q_0$
##### b. To find E-field at a point, place a $+1$ C charge there and calculate force
### B. Motion of Charge in Electric Field
#### 1. Kinematics
##### a. Force $F = qE$; Acceleration $a = qE/m$ 43, 44.b. Equations of motion ($v=u+at$, etc.) are valid if $E$ is uniform (constant acceleration)
#### 2. Trajectory
##### a. If velocity is parallel to $E$: Straight line motion (speed up or slow down)
##### b. If velocity is perpendicular/at an angle to $E$: Parabolic path (Projectile motion analogy)
  - i. Time of flight and Range formulas derived by replacing $g$ with $g_{eff} = qE/m$ (or $g \pm qE/m$)
### C. Electric Field Lines
#### 1. Properties
##### a. Originate from positive charge, terminate on negative charge
##### b. Never form closed loops
##### c. Never intersect (would imply two directions of force at one point)
##### d. Density of lines indicates field strength (closer lines = stronger field)
##### e. Number of lines is proportional to the magnitude of charge
#### 2. Behavior in Conductors
##### a. Electric field inside a conductor (electrostatics) is always zero
##### b. Field lines are always perpendicular to the surface of a conductor
## V. Electric Field Calculations (Continuous Distributions)
### A. Charged Ring
#### 1. On the Axis
##### a. Formula: $E = \frac{k Q x}{(R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}}$
##### b. At Center ($x=0$): Electric Field is zero due to symmetry
#### 2. Maxima
##### a. Field is maximum at distance $x = \pm R/\sqrt{2}$
### B. Line Charge (Wire/Thread)
#### 1. Finite Wire
##### a. Depends on angles subtended by ends ($\theta_1, \theta_2$)
##### b. Parallel component: $E_{||} = \frac{k\lambda}{r}(\cos\theta_2 - \cos\theta_1)$
##### c. Perpendicular component: $E_{\perp} = \frac{k\lambda}{r}(\sin\theta_1 + \sin\theta_2)$
#### 2. Infinite Wire
##### a. Angles $\theta_1 = \theta_2 = 90^\circ$
##### b. Formula: $E = \frac{2k\lambda}{r}$
#### 3. Semi-Infinite Wire
##### a. One angle $0^\circ$, other $90^\circ$
##### b. Field has components $k\lambda/r$ in both perpendicular and parallel directions
### C. Circular Arcs
#### 1. General Formula
##### a. On angle bisector: $E = \frac{2k\lambda}{R} \sin(\theta/2)$
#### 2. Specific Shapes
##### a. Semi-circle ($\theta = \pi$): $E = \frac{2kQ}{\pi R^2}$
##### b. Quarter-circle ($\theta = \pi/2$): $E = \frac{2\sqrt{2}kQ}{\pi R^2}$
## VI. Electric Dipole
### A. Structure and Moment
#### 1. Definition
##### a. System of two equal and opposite charges separated by a small distance ($2a$)
##### b. Total charge is zero (Monopole moment = 0)
#### 2. Dipole Moment ($\vec{p}$)
##### a. Vector quantity: $\vec{p} = q \times (2a)$
##### b. Direction: From Negative to Positive charge
### B. Electric Field Due to Dipole
#### 1. Axial Position (End-on)
##### a. Formula: $E_{axial} = \frac{2kp}{r^3}$
##### b. Direction: Parallel to dipole moment $\vec{p}$
#### 2. Equatorial Position (Broad-side on)
##### a. Formula: $E_{eq} = \frac{kp}{r^3}$
##### b. Direction: Anti-parallel to dipole moment $\vec{p}$
#### 3. General Point
##### a. Formula: $E = \frac{kp}{r^3} \sqrt{3\cos^2\theta + 1}$
##### b. Ratio of Axial to Equatorial field is $2:1$
### C. Dipole in External Uniform Field
#### 1. Forces and Torque
##### a. Net Force: $F_{net} = 0$ (in uniform field)
##### b. Torque: $\vec{\tau} = \vec{p} \times \vec{E} = pE \sin\theta$
##### c. Max Torque at $90^\circ$, Min Torque at $0^\circ$ or $180^\circ$
#### 2. Energy and Work
##### a. Potential Energy: $U = -\vec{p} \cdot \vec{E} = -pE \cos\theta$
##### b. Stable Equilibrium: $\theta = 0^\circ$ ($U_{min} = -pE$)
##### c. Unstable Equilibrium: $\theta = 180^\circ$ ($U_{max} = +pE$)
##### d. Work Done in rotating: $W = pE(\cos\theta_1 - \cos\theta_2)$
## VII. Electric Flux and Gauss's Law
### A. Electric Flux ($\phi$)
#### 1. Definition
##### a. Measure of field lines passing perpendicularly through an area
##### b. Formula: $\phi = \vec{E} \cdot \vec{A} = EA \cos\theta$
##### c. Scalar quantity
#### 2. Sign Convention
##### a. Outgoing flux is Positive
##### b. Incoming flux is Negative
#### 3. Projection Rule
##### a. Flux = Field $\times$ Projected Area (shadow area perpendicular to field)
### B. Gauss's Law
#### 1. Statement
##### a. Total flux through a closed surface is $1/\epsilon_0$ times the enclosed charge: $\oint \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{A} = \frac{Q_{in}}{\epsilon_0}$
#### 2. Gaussian Surface Properties
##### a. Can be any shape or size; Flux depends only on enclosed charge
##### b. If charge is outside, net flux through the surface is zero
##### c. Electric field in the integral is due to all charges (inside + outside), but Flux is due to inside charges only
### C. Applications of Gauss's Law
#### 1. Conducting Solid/Hollow Sphere (Shell)
##### a. Inside ($r < R$): $E = 0$
##### b. Outside ($r > R$): Behaves like a point charge, $E = \frac{kQ}{r^2}$
#### 2. Non-Conducting Solid Sphere (Uniform Charge)
##### a. Outside ($r > R$): $E = \frac{kQ}{r^2}$
##### b. Inside ($r < R$): Directly proportional to distance, $E = \frac{\rho r}{3\epsilon_0}$
#### 3. Infinite Sheet
##### a. Formula: $E = \frac{\sigma}{2\epsilon_0}$
##### b. Independent of distance from the sheet
#### 4. Solid Non-Conducting Cylinder
##### a. Inside ($r < R$): $E = \frac{\rho r}{2\epsilon_0}$
##### b. Outside ($r > R$): $E = \frac{\rho R^2}{2\epsilon_0 r}$ (inversely proportional to $r$)
## VIII. Charge Distribution on Parallel Plates
### A. Distribution Logic
#### 1. Facing Surfaces
##### a. Charges on facing inner surfaces of parallel plates are equal and opposite
#### 2. Outer Surfaces
##### a. Charges on the outermost surfaces must be equal
##### b. Formula: $Q_{outer} = \frac{\sum Q_{all\_plates}}{2}$
#### 3. Example
##### a. If plates have charges $Q_1$ and $Q_2$, outer faces get $(Q_1+Q_2)/2$, inner faces adjust to conserve charge
